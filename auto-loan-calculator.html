How Your Car Payment Is Calculated

Amount financed = vehicle price + sales tax − down payment − trade-in. The monthly payment uses the standard loan formula on that amount over your chosen term.

Example

$25,000 car, 8% sales tax, $5,000 down, 6.5% APR for 60 months: you finance $22,000, pay $430/month and about $3,830 in total interest.

Term length changes total cost

  • Longer terms lower the monthly payment but add interest and keep you owing more than the car is worth for longer.
  • Many states tax only the price after the trade-in credit. If yours does, the tax shown here is slightly high.
  • Dealer add-ons (extended warranty, GAP, paint protection) rolled into the loan raise both the payment and the interest — price them separately.
💡 Get a pre-approval from a bank or credit union before visiting the dealer. It sets a rate the dealer has to beat.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 20/4/10 rule for buying a car? ▼
Put at least 20% down, finance for no more than 4 years, and keep total vehicle costs (payment, insurance, fuel) under 10% of gross income. It is a conservative guideline, not a lender requirement.
What is a good APR for a car loan? ▼
Excellent credit (750+): 4–6%. Good credit (700+): 6–9%. Fair credit: 9–14%. Always compare multiple lenders.
Should I choose longer loan term? ▼
Longer terms lower monthly payments but increase total interest. 72-84 month loans also risk being 'underwater' (owing more than car's value).
Is 0% financing really free? ▼
0% APR is real but usually requires excellent credit and may mean giving up a cash discount. Always compare total cost both ways.
