How the Debt Payoff Calculation Works

Interest each month is balance × (rate ÷ 12). Your payment covers that first; the remainder cuts the balance. Extra money goes 100% to principal, which is why small additions have an outsized effect.

Example: $20,000 at 18%

Monthly paymentDebt-free inTotal interest
$4007 yrs 10 mo$17,245
$6003 yrs 11 mo$7,934

An extra $200 a month halves the time and saves over $9,300.

Avalanche vs snowball with several debts

  • Avalanche: pay minimums on everything, send all extra money to the highest-rate debt. Mathematically cheapest.
  • Snowball: send extra money to the smallest balance first. Costs a little more but quick wins keep many people going.

For several debts, enter the total balance and a weighted average rate here for a good approximation.

💡 If your payment barely exceeds the monthly interest, the calculator will warn you — that debt will take decades to clear and needs a higher payment, a lower rate, or consolidation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is debt consolidation a good idea? ▼
Only if the new loan's APR — including any origination fee — is lower than your current weighted rate, and you stop adding new debt to the cards you paid off.
Avalanche vs Snowball method? ▼
Avalanche (highest rate first) saves most money. Snowball (smallest balance first) provides motivation. Both work — pick what keeps you consistent.
Should I pay debt or invest? ▼
If debt rate is above 6-7%, pay debt first. Below 6%, investing often wins long-term. Always pay high-interest debt (10%+) before investing.
How to find extra money for debt? ▼
Cut subscriptions, meal prep, sell unused items, pick up gig work. Even $50-100 extra/month makes huge difference over time.
