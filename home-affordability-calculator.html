How Home Affordability Is Calculated

Lenders cap two debt-to-income (DTI) ratios. The front-end ratio keeps total housing cost — principal, interest, property tax, insurance and PMI (PITI) — at or below 28% of gross monthly income. The back-end ratio keeps housing plus all other debt payments at or below 36%. Your budget is whichever limit is lower.

The calculator then finds the highest home price whose full monthly cost fits that budget. If your down payment is under 20%, it adds private mortgage insurance at an estimated 0.6% of the loan per year.

Example

$80,000 income, $500/month car payment, $40,000 down, 7% for 30 years, 1.2% property tax, $1,500/yr insurance: housing budget is $1,867/month, which supports a home price of about $249,000. Your $40,000 is under 20% of that price, so PMI is included.

💡 Every $100 of monthly debt you pay off before applying raises your back-end budget by $100 a month — worth roughly $12,000 of home price at a 7% rate.

What lenders actually allow

  • Conventional loans often approve back-end DTI up to 45–50% with strong credit.
  • FHA loans can go higher with compensating factors.
  • Approval limits are not comfort limits. 28/36 leaves room for maintenance — budget 1–2% of the home's value per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this include closing costs? ▼
No. Closing costs typically run 2–5% of the purchase price and are paid in cash at closing, on top of your down payment.
What is the 28/36 rule? ▼
Housing costs (PITI) should not exceed 28% of gross income. Total debt payments should not exceed 36%.
How much down payment do I need? ▼
Minimum 3-5% for conventional, 3.5% for FHA. 20% avoids PMI and saves hundreds per month.
What affects how much I can borrow? ▼
Credit score, debt-to-income ratio, down payment, employment history, and current interest rates all affect loan amount.
