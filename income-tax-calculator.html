How Federal Income Tax Is Calculated (2026)

Taxable income = gross income − pre-tax deductions (401(k), HSA) − the standard deduction or your itemized deductions. Brackets then apply only to the slice of income inside each bracket. Moving into a higher bracket never reduces your take-home pay.

2026 standard deduction

  • Single / married filing separately: $16,100
  • Married filing jointly: $32,200
  • Head of household: $24,150

Example: a single filer earning $75,000 has $58,900 taxable income and owes about $7,670 in federal income tax — a 10.2% effective rate, 22% marginal rate.

Payroll taxes are separate: Social Security is 6.2% on wages up to $184,500 in 2026, and Medicare is 1.45% on all wages plus 0.9% above $200,000 (single). State and local income tax is not included here.

💡 Credits (child tax credit, education credits) reduce tax dollar for dollar and are not modelled here. Treat the result as an estimate, not a filed return.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 2026 standard deduction? ▼
$16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly and $24,150 head of household (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32).
Does a 401(k) contribution lower my taxes? ▼
Traditional 401(k) deferrals reduce federal taxable income. They do not reduce Social Security or Medicare tax.
What is effective vs marginal tax rate? ▼
Marginal = your highest bracket rate. Effective = total tax ÷ total income. Only income within each bracket is taxed at that rate.
How to reduce tax bill? ▼
Maximize 401k/IRA contributions, HSA, itemize if above standard deduction, harvest tax losses, and claim all eligible credits.
