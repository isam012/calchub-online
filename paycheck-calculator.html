How Your Paycheck Is Calculated

Your employer withholds four main items from each check. This calculator estimates each one with 2026 rules, then divides by the number of paychecks in a year.

  1. Pre-tax deductions — traditional 401(k) and HSA contributions come out first and lower your federal taxable income.
  2. Federal income tax — 2026 brackets applied to annual pay minus pre-tax deductions and the standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household).
  3. FICA — Social Security 6.2% on wages up to $184,500 and Medicare 1.45% on all wages (+0.9% above $200,000 single / $250,000 joint). 401(k) deferrals do not reduce FICA.
  4. State income tax — enter your state's approximate rate. Nine states have no wage income tax, including Texas, Florida, Nevada, Washington and Tennessee.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax is taken out of a $1,000 paycheck? ▼
It depends on annual income and filing status. For a single filer earning $26,000 a year ($1,000 biweekly), roughly $40 federal income tax and $77 FICA come out of each check, before state tax.
Why is my first paycheck of the year bigger or smaller? ▼
Usually because Social Security stops once you pass the $184,500 wage base, or because benefit deductions and 401(k) elections changed at the start of the year.
Does a 401(k) contribution reduce my paycheck dollar for dollar? ▼
No. A $100 traditional 401(k) contribution also lowers federal (and usually state) income tax, so take-home pay drops by less — about $78 in the 22% bracket.
Is biweekly or semi-monthly pay better? ▼
Annual pay is identical. Biweekly gives 26 smaller checks, including two months a year with three paydays; semi-monthly gives 24 larger checks on fixed dates.
