How Savings Growth Is Calculated

APY (annual percentage yield) already includes compounding, so the calculator converts it to an equivalent monthly rate: i = (1 + APY)1/12 − 1. Each month interest is added and your deposit lands at the end of the month.

Future value = start × (1+i)months + deposit × [(1+i)months − 1] ÷ i

APY vs APR

APR is the simple annual rate; APY is what you actually earn after compounding. A 4.40% APR compounded daily is about a 4.50% APY. Banks advertise APY on savings — compare APY to APY.

Where savings rates stand

Online high-yield savings accounts and money-market accounts typically pay several times the rate of a big-bank standard savings account. Rates follow central-bank policy and change often; check the bank's current APY and confirm FDIC (US), FSCS (UK) or CDIC (Canada) protection.

Inflation

The "today's dollars" figure divides the result by (1 + inflation)years. If your APY is below inflation, your savings lose purchasing power even as the balance rises.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I keep in savings? ▼
A common rule is an emergency fund of 3–6 months of essential expenses, more if your income is irregular. Savings beyond that, for goals 5+ years away, are usually better invested.
Is interest on savings taxable? ▼
In the US, yes — interest is taxed as ordinary income and reported on Form 1099-INT. In the UK, the Personal Savings Allowance covers some interest; ISAs are tax-free.
How often is interest paid? ▼
Most savings accounts compound daily and pay monthly. Because the calculator uses APY, the compounding frequency is already accounted for.
